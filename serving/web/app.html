About this forecast

What you are looking at

Predicted chlorophyll-a concentration at 300 m resolution, 1 to 7 days ahead, for the area you select. Chlorophyll-a is a proxy for algal biomass — the standard satellite signal for tracking cyanobacterial blooms.

Where the data comes from

Sentinel-3 OLCI Level-2 water products. The mission itself is near-real time — EUMETSAT publishes NRT products within about 3 hours of acquisition, and consolidated NTC products within 30 days. What lags is the free Microsoft Planetary Computer mirror this build reads, which republishes the same products roughly 6–7 months later (measured August 2026). So a forecast here is anchored on the most recent day that mirror actually holds, never on today, and that date is always shown. Operational latency is a matter of changing the ingest source to Copernicus Data Space, not of changing the model.

How to read the map

Colour runs from 1 mg/m³ (deep blue) to 100 mg/m³ (red) on a fixed scale, so two different lakes are directly comparable. Grey means water the sensor could not see through cloud — it is missing data, not clean water. Two thirds of a typical day is grey, which is why a forecast is built from a fortnight of passes. The inspect tool reads the predicted value under a click, at every lead time at once.

How good is it?

Measured lake-by-lake against both "tomorrow looks like today" and a seasonal climatology. On a lake it has never seen it beats persistence by a small margin and still loses to climatology. The full evaluation, including the failures, is on the results dashboard.

Limits worth knowing

300 m pixels cannot resolve small ponds or narrow rivers. The water mask is derived from the fortnight being fetched, so a cloudy period maps less water than really exists. The forecast is computed on a local UTM grid and drawn onto a Web Mercator basemap; selections are capped at 80 km partly because below that the difference between the two projections stays under one pixel. And satellite chlorophyll is not a cyanotoxin measurement: confirm with in-situ sampling before any public-health decision.
